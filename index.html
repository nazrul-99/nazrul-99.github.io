Biography

I'm a lecturer in computer science at Southeast University, Dhaka. My research spans vision-language models and low-resource NLP. One question runs through both: what is a model's failure actually made of? In three projects so far the accepted explanation named the wrong cause. Correcting it changed which fix works. The research threads below show where.

The questions those projects left open are the ones I want to pursue next. In vision-language models, one question is how to give a model geometry it can reason over rather than more examples of the right answers. The other is how to evaluate spatial reasoning so that a score identifies the capability, not a bias in the benchmark. In low-resource NLP, the question is what large language models actually depend on when the input is noisy, non-Latin, and underrepresented in pretraining. The defenses worth testing are the ones that dependence predicts.

Research

  1. Geometry in the token, not in the pipeline

    Submitted to AAAI 2027

    A VLM answers "is the chair in front of the sofa" from a grid of patch features that carry no position, so it answers from word statistics. We lift each patch to a metric 3D point and cluster the points into voxel tokens with a soft assignment. The question-answering loss trains the depth, the camera, and the grouping together.

    Reported
    Spatial reasoning improves with more spatial QA data, or with detection and segmentation estimators added at inference.
    Found
    Language loss alone learns a text shortcut: 41.5% on binary relations, below chance. Moving geometry into the tokenizer reaches 81.33% on SpatialRGPT-Bench at 1B parameters in 3.6 GB, with no region proposal at inference.

    Paper

  2. What a benchmark score identifies

    Submitted to ICCIT 2026

    MMVP's 6% for LLaVA-1.5 fits a model that cannot see. It fits equally a model that sees fine but always picks option (a). A single score is consistent with both readings until a control separates them. I build the cheapest control that does, then report what the published number was actually tracking.

    Reported
    LLaVA-1.5 scores 6.0% on MMVP, below the 25% random floor. The literature reads this as a perceptual deficit inherited from CLIP.
    Found
    93% of its failures pick the same slot whatever the image shows. On answers that survive reversal its accuracy is 0.70, indistinguishable from the model scoring 33.3.

    Paper

  3. What a low-resource model is keying on

    In preparation, 2027

    Bangla encoders are evaluated on clean text. Bangla outside curated corpora is not clean. Rather than report that noise hurts, I control the comparison until the damage can be attributed to something, then test whether that attribution predicts which defense works.

    Reported
    Character noise degrades Bangla encoders because it fragments subword tokenization.
    Found
    With coverage held fixed, fragmentation predicts almost none of the loss (r = 0.05). The model is anchored to memorized entity spellings by 9.3 to 1 over context. Failing to find the entity at all accounts for 86% of the damage. Character augmentation recovers 14.3 F1, whereas FGSM recovers nothing outside seed noise.

    Paper

  4. Adversarial Robustness in Low-Resource NLP (Bangla)

    2025 · PyTorch

    I ran character-, word-, and noise-level attacks against Bangla language models to measure where robustness breaks. That work is the diagnostic groundwork for the NER study above.

  5. Multiclass SQL Injection Detection via Hybrid CNN–Transformer

    2024–2025 · PyTorch

    I built a 1D CNN–TinyBERT classifier for multiclass SQL-injection payloads. Because the classes are heavily imbalanced, I evaluated it with macro-F1 and PR-AUC under realistic adversarial scenarios.

    Project

  6. Lightweight LLM and Reinforcement Learning for Text-to-SQL

    2023 · PyTorch

    I built a TinyBERT text-to-SQL system on Spider. Reinforcement learning from execution feedback improved logical correctness without a larger model.

    Project

  7. Undergraduate thesis: BCI-Oriented EEG Signal Analysis and Classification

    2022–2023 · TensorFlow

    I built end-to-end ERP detection on BCI Competition data, where cross-subject generalization was the bottleneck. The pipeline used FIR/notch/CAR preprocessing, PSD/WPD/STFT features, and NCA and Relief-F selection. The CNN reached 92% cross-subject accuracy. Ablations attributed the gain to pipeline stages rather than the model.
